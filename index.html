Two versions→Same time, split traffic→Compare results→Keep or learn

An A/B test is a way to compare two versions of a page, to see which one performs better. Half of the visitors see the current version, and the other half see the new one, at the same time.

Then we look at the results, for example the conversion rate, to decide which version to keep.

For example, if we think users hesitate because they can’t see if their size is in stock, the new version would show stock availability, and the current version would stay the same. If the new version gets more purchases, we keep it. If not, we learn from it and form a new hypothesis.

Si te preguntan cuánto dura un test

It depends on the traffic. We need enough visitors to be confident in the result, so we don’t decide too early. The analyst usually defines that.

Si te preguntan si los corriste vos

I haven’t run the tests myself, but I understand how they work, and I design the variants from the hypothesis.

Vocabulario

Control: la versión actual, la que no cambia.

⭐ Huntech: versión única (unos 4 min con las decisiones, unos 2 min sin ellas)
Context→Role→Heuristic analysis→3 areas→5 design decisions→Delivery→Results→Lesson

1. Contexto y rol

Huntech is a New Zealand outdoor retailer specialising in technical hunting gear and equipment. Their eCommerce experience was no longer keeping up with the needs of the business and its users, which created several opportunities to improve the shopping experience and conversion.

My role was to lead the UI redesign, working within a multidisciplinary team under our Design Lead.

2. Análisis y foco

Before designing, I conducted a heuristic analysis of the website to find the biggest opportunities. Rather than redesigning the whole website, we focused on the PLP and PDP, where customers compare products and make purchase decisions. The goal was to reduce purchase uncertainty and help users decide with more confidence.

I grouped the findings into three areas: Usability, Comprehension and Merchandising. From these, I identified five design opportunities: Product Confidence, Product Discovery, Mobile Filtering, Product Communication and Shopping Efficiency.

3. Decisiones (solo si hay tiempo o si te piden detalle)

Let me show you how I turned each one into a design decision.

1. Product Confidence (Comprehension). Out-of-stock products were still visible when filtering by size, size selection patterns were inconsistent, and the Size Guide had low visibility. I introduced real-time stock visibility, standardised sizing patterns and an integrated and visible Size Guide.

Los productos agotados seguían visibles al filtrar por talle, los patrones de selección de talles eran inconsistentes y la guía de talles tenía poca visibilidad. Incorporé visibilidad de stock en tiempo real, patrones de talles estandarizados y una guía de talles integrada y visible.

2. Product Discovery (Merchandising). The header hierarchy was unclear, the menu’s category structure was text-heavy, and search had no guidance. I introduced a clear header hierarchy, visual category tiles and guided search with Best Sellers.

La jerarquía del header no era clara, la estructura de categorías del menú tenía demasiado texto y el buscador no ofrecía guía. Incorporé una jerarquía clara en el header, tiles visuales de categorías y un buscador guiado con Best Sellers.

3. Mobile Filtering (Usability). Filtering was fragmented across multiple steps, there was no sorting option available, and touch targets were too small on mobile. I introduced a single-step filtering experience, integrated filtering and sorting, and 48px touch-safe targets.

El filtrado estaba fragmentado en varios pasos, no había opción de ordenar y las áreas táctiles eran demasiado pequeñas en el celular. Incorporé una experiencia de filtrado en un solo paso, filtrado y ordenamiento integrados, y áreas táctiles de 48px seguras para el dedo.

4. Product Communication (Comprehension). Technical benefits were only explained through text, and Add to Cart was not always visible. I introduced visual icons for key product benefits and a sticky Add to Cart button.

Los beneficios técnicos solo se explicaban con texto y el botón Add to Cart no siempre estaba visible. Incorporé íconos visuales para los beneficios clave del producto y un botón Add to Cart fijo (sticky).

Part of my workflow→colour and contrast→sizes and states→structure and content→handoff notes + example for screen readers→AI

Accessibility is part of my normal workflow, not just a final check.

I check that the colours have enough contrast, following WCAG. I also don’t use colour alone to show meaning. I look at the size of text, icons and buttons, the typography and the form states, like hover, error, success and disabled.

I also care about the structure and the content: a clear heading hierarchy, alt text and clear copy. I make sure the design works on different screen sizes and with zoom, and when there is motion, I follow standards like letting users pause or reduce it.

In the handoff, I leave notes for the developers, and sometimes examples or a working prototype. For screen readers, the code is what matters, so I work with the developers on it. For example, a button with only an icon needs a label, like “Add to cart”, so I add it in the handoff notes.

Today, AI tools also help me to find accessibility issues and fix them faster.
